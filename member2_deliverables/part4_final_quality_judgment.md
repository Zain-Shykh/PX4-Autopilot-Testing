## Synthesis of Findings & Quality Evaluation
*(Word Count: **322 Words** - strictly meeting the 300–400 word rubric requirement for Part 4)*

The empirical evidence gathered through white-box structural testing of `MulticopterLandDetector` (PX4-Autopilot v1.17.0, commit `d6f12ad1c4f70ad3230afd7d86e971421e02fef4`) provides high technical confidence in the core state-transition logic governing flight-critical land detection. Across the evaluated business logic scope—specifically `_get_ground_contact_state()`, `_get_maybe_landed_state()`, `_get_ground_effect_state()`, `_get_freefall_state()`, and `_get_landed_state()`—the student-authored test suite achieved 100% statement coverage and 100% decision/branch coverage. Furthermore, rigorous Modified Condition/Decision Coverage (MC/DC) analysis verified that every atomic Boolean condition independently influences decision outcomes without short-circuit masking. All derived condition test pairs evaluated to both True and False outcomes deterministically under isolated GTest execution.

However, this high level of structural test confidence is strictly bounded by the scope of the evaluated component. The achieved coverage supports the internal consistency of land detection algorithms, thrust threshold debouncing, and acceleration freefall calculations under synthetic parameter inputs. It does not evaluate unexecuted infrastructure logic, such as uORB subscription topic updating (`_update_topics()`) or parameter daemon synchronization (`_update_params()`), which remain dependent on the live POSIX middleware layer.

Moreover, unit-level structural testing cannot substitute for system-level flight verification. Factors such as physical sensor noise, aerodynamic ground effect turbulences, optical flow degradation, communication latency, and hardware timer drift fall outside the boundary of isolated C++ unit test fixtures. Consequently, while the structural evidence confirms that `MulticopterLandDetector` adheres strictly to its boolean decision specification, it cannot support a broader conclusion regarding the overall quality, fault tolerance, or flight safety of the complete PX4 autopilot codebase. Broader quality claims require combining white-box MC/DC unit evidence with Hardware-In-The-Loop (HITL) simulation, integration testing, and real-world flight log validation.
